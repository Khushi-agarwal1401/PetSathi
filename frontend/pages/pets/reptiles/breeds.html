Reptile Categories

Companion reptiles fall into a few broad groups, each with distinct bodies, habitats, and husbandry needs.

Geckos

Small, agile climbers and ground dwellers with specialized skin, sensitive eyes, and species-specific humidity needs.

Leopard Gecko • Crested Gecko • African Fat-Tailed Gecko

Lizards

A diverse group ranging from desert baskers to tropical tree dwellers, with widely varying heat, UVB, and diet requirements.

Bearded Dragon • Blue-Tongued Skink • Chameleon

Snakes

Limbless carnivores with calm temperaments, secure escape-proof enclosures, and whole-prey feeding schedules.

Shelled reptiles split between aquatic turtles needing swimming and basking space, and terrestrial tortoises needing dry, spacious habitats.

Key Selection Factors

Choosing the Right Reptile

Before welcoming a reptile home, consider how its space, climate, diet, temperament, and lifespan fit into your daily life.

Space & Enclosure

Plan for the adult size, not the hatchling. Provide a secure enclosure with the right orientation—wide for ground dwellers, tall for arboreal species—and room for hides, water, and a thermal gradient.

Temperature & Humidity

Reptiles depend on external heat. Use thermostats and digital hygrometers to create a warm basking zone, a cooler retreat, and species-appropriate humidity.

Feeding Requirements

Match the diet to the species: live insects, fresh greens, fruit, or humanely sourced frozen-thawed prey. Gut-load and dust feeders with calcium as advised.

Handling & Temperament

Some reptiles tolerate gentle, brief handling; others prefer observation. Learn body language, avoid overhandling, and let new pets settle in before regular sessions.

Long-Term Commitment

Many reptiles live for decades. Budget for enclosure upgrades, heating and UVB replacement, food, and exotic veterinary care over the animal's full lifespan.

Sourcing & Vet Care

Choose a responsible breeder or rescue, confirm the animal is captive-bred and healthy, and locate an experienced reptile veterinarian before you bring it home.

Important Care Reminder

Every Reptile Species Is Different

There is no single “reptile setup”. A bearded dragon, a crested gecko, a ball python, and a red-eared slider all need different enclosure sizes, temperatures, humidity levels, lighting, and diets. Always research the exact species—and ideally the exact morph or locality—before adjusting heat, UVB, or feeding schedules.

  • Habitat Desert, tropical, arboreal, aquatic, or terrestrial—each species needs a different enclosure and substrate.
  • Temperature Basking spots, cool zones, and nighttime drops must match the species' natural range.
  • Humidity Too dry or too damp can cause shedding problems, respiratory infections, or shell issues.
  • Diet Insectivore, herbivore, omnivore, or carnivore—never assume another reptile's diet will suit yours.
